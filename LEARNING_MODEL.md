# Learning model · Sprich 2.3

## The learner and goal

The learner now identifies as near-beginner in speaking and wants mixed everyday, university, and work situations. The first goal is to understand a short question, say a useful sentence, and ask something back. The interface does not infer a CEFR level or promise a date for job readiness.

## Start Speaking: a supported entry point

The default path has 48 lessons in twelve modules and 250 vocabulary entries. Each lesson starts with a phrase to say immediately, then words, a short language-focus note, sentence patterns, a bilingual dialogue, three guided replies, a variation, and a finish/reflection screen. All beginner instructions and models have English support.

### Difficulty is a property of the task

Every lesson carries a level (Starter, A1, A2, B1), a topic, a speaking skill, and advisory preparation. The level describes what the lesson asks for — repeat a model, manage a familiar transaction, add a reason, or explain and compare — using the CEFR spoken-interaction descriptors as editorial guidance. Completing lessons records practice. The application never infers that the learner *is* A1 or A2, and never converts lesson completion into a proficiency claim.

Support is chosen separately from difficulty. **Guided** keeps the English meaning and a model answer one tap away; **less help** hides both until asked. Support changes the amount of scaffolding, not the evidence rules: an independent self-rating still requires an aloud attempt with no model reveal for that task.

### The path guides without locking

Lessons are ordered into five stages. The recommendation resumes an unfinished lesson first, then offers a guided retry when the learner marked two or more answers for another try, then the first unpractised lesson whose preparation is already done — each with a short stated reason. Prerequisites appear as "Useful first" links and are validated to precede their lesson in the path, but never block it. A learner who already knows a topic can skip straight to it.

Warm-up repetition is unscored. Guided replies use one explicit reflection rather than a three-item rubric: practise again, needed help, or said it on my own. Recording is optional, capped at 60 seconds per response, and stored locally. Where a lesson has two model conversations, practising it again uses the other one, so repetition is re-practice rather than rereading.

Words are learnt in small batches. Recognition of a meaning, spoken retrieval of German, and use in an aloud sentence are separate modes with separate histories. Seeing a word, bookmarking it, or exporting a lesson is not recall evidence. Revealing before trying prevents an independent result for that attempt.

Today keeps one concrete lesson assignment and up to five word targets for the day. Due spoken-recall words are offered first. Extra time can go into another variation or a Gemini conversation; the app does not assign time merely for finishing a sequence.

## Lesson-specific Gemini practice

Every lesson can become a portable practice pack before, during, or after study. The pack includes the actual lesson’s words, meanings, patterns, chosen dialogue, variation, level target, and help setting. Instructions request a short warm-up, then natural back-and-forth with short questions, level-appropriate rescue help, and at most two corrections after several learner replies.

Only explicitly checked profile fields are added. A learner can copy/share the pack or download text, then continue in Gemini Live on their phone. Sprich saves and stops any active lesson recording before handoff. The external conversation is not observed or graded by Sprich; returned notes and minutes are self-reported.

## Workplace path: one mission, a complete practice loop

1. **A concrete goal:** understand the situation and what you need to communicate.
2. **Input:** hear a short example, answer two questions, then use the transcript to check meaning.
3. **Retrieval:** try a phrase from memory before revealing the model. Explicitly report whether you needed help.
4. **Rhythm:** listen to a short line, repeat it, and compare your recording. This is unscored practice.
5. **Interaction:** answer a sequence of relevant questions aloud. Use a clarification or a simpler question when needed.
6. **Reflection:** replay the answer and check three task-specific statements. Pick one thing to improve.
7. **Reuse:** give another answer with less support, then schedule the next retrieval attempt.

The selectable workplace path begins with interview practice. Other missions cover instructions, project stories, and small talk. Each mission has multiple prompts and listening examples. An availability response changes the next interviewer question through an explicit choice; the app does not pretend to semantically understand arbitrary speech.

The workplace course has twelve missions, 36 original dialogues, 108 comprehension questions, and 120 curated phrases. Its six stages allow the learner to choose any situation. A stage records topics practised, not a CEFR level earned. Every mission has a complete short route with at least two speaking turns and a retry.

## Resources become active practice

Each of the fifteen source entries has a specific follow-up activity. The learner visits the source, returns, and saves a written German response or recording. Opening the link or checking “I spoke aloud” by itself does not complete a resource activity.

Resource responses are **unscored practice**. They cannot inflate independent speaking or listening percentages. Outside-study minutes are optional self-reports and are shown separately from the app's measured active time. Bookmarks, difficulty ratings, and reported availability shape later recommendations.

Personal phrases preserve their English meaning, source, and an original example. Matching German/English entries have stable identities across devices. If an entry matches a core phrase, the learner's source and example are retained as an annotation while recall continues under the existing core identity.

## German first, English when needed

New lessons present German instructions, prompts, model responses, and comprehension questions. **Meaning** reveals the English counterpart. The optional always-visible setting supports learners who need both languages together.

Question/instruction translations support understanding and are distinguished from answer models. A translation of a listening transcript is reading support. Revealing a speaking model is answer assistance. Hidden model translations do not count as viewed simply because the learner selected always-visible English.

## Evidence types

| Evidence | How it is evaluated | What it supports |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Listening answers | Fixed answer keys, one saved submission per question | Practice comprehension on these examples |
| Reading-supported listening | Same answer keys, assistance recorded | Supported practice; excluded from unassisted listening percentage |
| Phrase recall | Learner compares their attempt to a model | A self-rated review schedule |
| Speaking | Learner checks task criteria and rates their attempt | Self-reported independence and support needs |
| Recording | Measured recorder duration; audio available for replay | A record of the response, including pauses |
| Typed draft | Saved text, explicitly labelled typed | Preparation and reflection; not independent spoken evidence |
| Shadowing | Explicit practice confirmation | Repetition; no independent-production credit |
| Resource response | Saved text or recording, labelled unscored | Evidence of a follow-up practice activity |
| Outside study | Learner-entered minutes | Self-reported time, separate from measured activity |

No nonempty text, number of words, turn count, or Finish button automatically earns a language success. Each task has a stable attempt ID. Duplicate submission or import does not multiply results.

## How self-assessment works

- **Practise again:** you could not communicate the answer comfortably.
- **Needed help:** you needed a model, a hint, or substantial support.
- **Answered independently:** you practised aloud, met the task's three criteria, and did not use an answer model.

Showing an answer hint prevents an independent grade for that attempt. Asking for a question to be repeated or simplified is a legitimate repair strategy; it is not automatically treated as failed spoken production.

The phrase-use checklist appears after the learner reports or records a spoken response. Only phrases they explicitly report using are attached to that production evidence. Recording again after revealing those phrases is marked assisted.

These are transparent self-assessments, not automatic grammar or pronunciation judgements. Human feedback and actual conversations remain valuable checks on the learner's judgement.

## Scheduling

Recall and production have separate evidence histories. Default successful recall intervals are **1, 3, 7, 14, 30, and 60 days**. An unsuccessful attempt returns in about **4 hours**; an assisted attempt returns within **one day**.

Independent repetitions advance a schedule only when due, on a different study day, and at least 20 hours after the previous counted success. The normal one-day due time is still respected. Immediate repeats and early practice do not postpone the review or establish spaced production.

An assisted overdue review receives a future due date. An early assisted review can shorten an existing future interval but does not postpone it. Finishing a phrase review does not finish its parent mission or advance the mission's dialogue variation.

The phrase review queue uses recall due dates. A phrase first used in conversation can enter that queue through its production evidence. The separate spoken-use badge requires at least two spaced independent production days; typed responses do not count.

Intervals are implementation defaults, not research-proven optimums for every person. The current planner prioritises due phrases and new mission phrases, and revisits missions where recent self-assessments indicate more help was needed.

## Time and progress

- Count time only while a practice session is visible, unpaused, and recently active.
- Pause counting after 60 seconds without activity; recording keeps the session active.
- Cap a delayed timer tick to avoid charging long browser suspensions as study time.
- Save compact per-minute checkpoints, split at local day boundaries.
- Union overlapping intervals so two windows cannot double-count the same active time.
- Calculate seven-day totals from dated intervals, rather than a lifetime counter called “week”.

The suggested 90-minute routine is: 10 minutes recall, 15 listening, 10 shadowing, 30 conversation, 15 reduced-support repetition, and 10 reflection/reuse. It can be split across the day. A completed mission does not automatically award its suggested duration.

Today assigns actual phrase IDs, a mission, and an accessible source within that budget. The assignment persists for the day, so completing a source does not immediately replace it with another unfinished item. The learner can change the focus. Short budgets use original in-app listening when no suitable external activity fits. Due review work, resource responses, mission attempts, and reflections each have their own completion conditions.

## Useful milestones

- Introduce your studies and experience in 60–90 seconds without a script.
- Explain real availability and handle a follow-up question.
- Ask for clarification and confirm an action and deadline.
- Tell a short project story with your own contribution and result.
- Return a question in small talk and arrange a next step.
- Recall useful phrases on later days with less assistance.

The Progress page shows the underlying evidence and recorded answers. Compare similar tasks and use a fresh real-world conversation or recording every couple of weeks to check transfer.
